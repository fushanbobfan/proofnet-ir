# v0.11 search-redundancy design

Status: active development
Baseline release: v0.10.0

## Purpose

v0.11 answers the project's founding question instead of extending the
library: does a proof-net representation reduce proof-search redundancy for
a proof assistant? The v0.10 experiments answered it only for matched MLL
tasks with a supplied connective skeleton, a budget unit that the methods do
not share, and a toy sequent baseline; their reports say so. v0.11 states
the question as three falsifiable measurements, each preregistered before
its data is produced and reported whichever way it comes out. A negative
answer closes the program; it is not a failure of the program.

## Step 1: measure the redundancy

Redundancy has an exact meaning here: distinct cut-free sequent derivations
that differ only in the order of rule applications denote the same proof net.
For a task, a one-sided sequent `⊢ F₁, …, Fₖ` of unit-free MLL formulas, fix
the occurrence forest of the formulas and count, treating every occurrence as
distinct and absorbing exchange:

- `D_all`: derivations in the plain calculus (axiom, tensor with every
  context split, par, any rule order);
- `D_weak`: derivations of the committed focused baseline
  (`scripts/focused_search.py`): the par phase is one deterministic step,
  then a tensor is chosen and its context split;
- `D_foc`: derivations of the strictly focused calculus, where the focus
  persists through the positive subformulas of the chosen tensor;
- `N`: correct proof nets of the sequent, the axiom linkings of the forest
  accepted by `Certificate.check`.

The ratios `D_all / N`, `D_weak / N`, and `D_foc / N` are the redundancy
factors of the three search spaces relative to the net space; `D_all / N`
is the mean number of sequentializations of a net of the task. The counts
are exact, by memoized recursion over sub-sequents with atom-balance
pruning, and are cross-checked on the small tasks by an independent
brute-force enumerator. Every count is a whole number; no sampling.

Corpus: the 1,000 committed matched tasks (`experiments/matched-v0.1`) and
the 180 held-out model tasks (`experiments/model-v0.2`), each with its
depth, atom count, conclusion count, and repeated-name flag. A task whose
exact count exceeds the preregistered state budget is reported as excluded
with its partial bound; exclusions are counted, never silently dropped.

Preregistered hypotheses:

- H1: `D_foc / N > 1` on more than half of the tasks: focusing does not
  remove all rule-order redundancy;
- H2: the median of `D_all / N` grows with the atom count faster than
  linearly across the three size classes;
- H3: `D_foc / D_weak < 1` on more than half of the tasks: strict focusing
  removes redundancy the committed baseline keeps.

The result is the distribution of the ratios by size class, with medians,
quartiles, and the fraction of tasks where each ratio equals 1. Success is
a correct number; no advantage is claimed from it.

## Step 2: fair matched search

Only after Step 1: compare a net-space search with the focused sequent
search under equal information and a shared budget unit. Both arms receive
the same input (the sequent alone, or the connective skeleton for both);
the budget is counted in checker or kernel calls and wall-clock, not in
method-specific units; the corpus is held out and includes negatives; the
hypothesis, budget, and stopping rule are preregistered before the search
is implemented. If the net-space search shows no advantage at equal
information, the answer for MLL is no, and Step 3 is not run.

## Step 3: skeleton-free model study

Only after a positive Step 2: repeat the 180-task model study without the
supplied skeleton, or with the skeleton given to both arms, measuring valid
proofs per token at a fixed budget.

## Beyond MLL

Proof nets exist cleanly for multiplicative linear logic. A proof assistant
at the scale of Lean and Mathlib adds dependent types, unification, and
rewriting, for which no proof-net theory exists; the LeanProp bridge covers
a propositional fragment only. Reaching the founding question at that scale
requires a different representation, for example hierarchical proof graphs
over elaborated terms, and is a new research program with its own
hypothesis and corpus. It starts only after Step 2 has answered whether
net-space search helps where the theory is clean, and it is designed as an
experiment first, not as a library.

## Release gates

- [ ] Step 1 preregistration committed before any count is produced;
- [ ] exact redundancy counts on the matched and model corpora, with the

- [ ] Step 1 report with the hypotheses answered;
- [ ] Step 2 preregistration, then its implementation and report;
- [ ] Step 3 only on a positive Step 2;
